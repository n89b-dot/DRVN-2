# Provider dispute and late-refund recovery

Provider dispute events enter a private journal keyed by Stripe dispute ID. Open and won/warning-closed basket changes commit atomically. A terminal callback received before its created callback remains terminal. A new provider dispute can reopen an earlier resolved in-app case. A complete, exact-currency basket can enter lost-dispute booking under a five-minute lease; retry verifies its ledger before completing. Expired loss leases appear in worker health. Unknown mappings remain retryable; partial, conflicting or over-50-order baskets create a deduplicated, alerted operator recovery and freeze eligible orders without guessing a full-basket refund.

Late refund failures leave a buyer refund outstanding and may require repayment of a reversed seller transfer. The admin Payments page now separates **Replace refund** (an explicit money action) from **Close case** (verification and bookkeeping).

1. Reconcile the original refund and seller reversal in Stripe. If seller repayment is owed, complete the approved recredit first. Its transfer must match the original seller account, exact amount, currency and test/live mode, have an unreversed balance transaction, and have been created after the compensation record. Include exact metadata `order_id`, `refund_operation_id`, `recredit_adjustment_id` and `refund_reference`. DRVN checks the original reversal too; uncertainty keeps the case open. A proven zero obligation needs no transfer.
2. Choose **Replace refund**, enter the seller transfer ID if needed, and record the audit reason. Verification saves immutable, uniquely attributed evidence. The database atomically releases only the definitively failed reservation and creates a replacement linked to that compensation record. Its new provider key and seller transfer target are permanent. It does not change the order's original transfer.
3. Repeated submissions and workers resume that replacement. A definitively failed attempt before settlement may receive a new attempt; an uncertain result never does. A replacement that later fails enters its own recovery case. Resolve that child case before its parent.
4. Choose **Close case** after settlement. Closure requires verified seller evidence and a successfully settled linked buyer refund (or its recovered successor). Notes and write-offs cannot erase either obligation. The historical seller proof remains valid after the replacement legitimately reverses that repayment transfer.

Ordinary refund initiation is blocked while a late-failure recovery is open. Other in-flight refunds must finish before a replacement can start. If intervening partial refunds changed the calculated seller share, the action refuses to guess a rounding or multi-transfer allocation. Such cases still require an approved reconciliation procedure. Seller recredit creation is not automated by this action; hosted Connect acceptance remains a release gate.

Provider fields and retrieval behavior were checked against Stripe's [Transfer object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/transfers/object) and [Retrieve a transfer](https://docs.stripe.com/api/transfers/retrieve) documentation. Local guards and mocks test admission, reservation, dispatch and replay; they do not prove hosted settlement.

The provider-boundary claim rechecks the available balance, seller allocation and other unresolved operations under the order lock before dispatch. Stripe-dashboard refunds issued after that boundary are external activity: operators must coordinate dashboard changes with app recovery and reconcile incoming provider facts. New unlinked provider facts during recovery are retained in the event journal and an unresolved case, rather than silently assigned to the replacement.
