# COUNTER — Concept 13 for DRVN

**Thesis:** *The deal is the design.* Buying a used part from another enthusiast is a negotiation — offer, counter, banter, handshake — and every surface of this concept is organized around making, receiving, and closing offers. The visual system (warm stone, two conversation colors, offer bubbles, the handshake seam) exists to serve the deal-making, not to be a metaphor.

**Generator family:** Interaction. Where eight prior concepts asked "what document or institution is this product?", COUNTER asks "what conversation shape IS this product?" The answer is the haggle. The signature components are the **offer bubble** (an amount-first chat message with a lifecycle state) and the **handshake band** (a full-width close where the buyer's terracotta and the seller's moss meet at a woven seam).

## Reference lineage (real, researched)

Researched on Mobbin during this build:

- **Etsy — Make an offer sheet** ([Mobbin screen](https://mobbin.com/screens/6516a2eb-6304-4e5a-acd7-c06603f7a50c)): plain-language framing ("the seller is free to turn down any offer"), a single amount field, and a soft behavioral nudge ("be thoughtful about your offer"). COUNTER borrows the tone-setting copy around the amount field — the composer hint that anchors expectations before you type.
- **Depop — Make an offer** ([Mobbin screen](https://mobbin.com/screens/2135e249-6484-4728-959a-7880867f1f7b)): discount presets with a "Recommended" anchor and the 24-hour acceptance rule. COUNTER's "recent similar sales £1,100–1,200" hint and the counter-expiry countdown are direct descendants of this pattern — anchoring plus urgency, kept friendly.
- **GOAT — Place your offer** ([Mobbin screen](https://mobbin.com/screens/46ec9498-fbcd-474c-9115-b14e41cd1b61)): offers carry full cost breakdown and a deliberate commit gesture (swipe to place). COUNTER's equivalent deliberate moment is the Accept action gated behind a sticky deal header, and the payment stub that itemizes the agreed figure after the handshake.
- **eBay Best Offer / Vinted messaging (product knowledge, not imagined):** both prove the core insight — offer flows fail when they live in modal purgatory disconnected from conversation. Vinted embeds offers *inside* the message thread as styled price cards; eBay's counteroffer history is a table bolted onto a listing. COUNTER takes Vinted's side of that argument to its conclusion: the thread is the primary surface and offers ARE messages, with amount, note, timestamp, and state in one bubble.
- **Messaging design systems** (iMessage/WhatsApp conventions): right = me, left = them, asymmetric corner radii pointing at the author, centered system rows for non-party events. COUNTER keeps all three conventions untouched so the negotiation reads instantly, then adds the one thing chat lacks: a state chip (Open / Countered / Accepted / Expired) because an offer, unlike a text, has a lifecycle.

## Why interaction-first serves the marketplace mission

DRVN's mission is the marketplace: selection, easy listing, easy buying. In a peer-to-peer parts market, *the price is almost never the listed price* — dealing is not a feature of the marketplace, it is the marketplace's liquidity mechanism. A design that makes offers first-class (composer on the listing equal to Buy; hover-reveal "Make an offer" on every card; a deals rail with live states) directly increases the number of conversations started, and conversations are what convert. Provenance ("came off my E46 track car") and seller credibility (★ 4.8 · 26 sales · responds ~20 min) appear exactly where a buyer's thumb hesitates — beside the price, inside the seller card — as supporting trust context, never as the headline.

## Iteration passes (performed)

1. **Pass 1 — Validation tone.** First draft of the counter validation rejected out-of-band amounts with "Invalid amount." Finding: a terse error in a bubble thread reads like the platform scolding you mid-banter and breaks the good-natured register. Rewrote to negotiation logic in plain speech: "A counter should land between £1,120 and £1,150 — otherwise you're moving backwards," paired with a gentle field nudge instead of a red border.
2. **Pass 2 — State legibility across the thread.** Initially every historical bubble kept an "Open" chip and the thread read as four simultaneous live offers. Finding: only the *latest* offer can be open; the deal is a chain, not a pile. Added `demoteOpenChips()` so sending or receiving a counter flips all prior chips to "Countered," making the thread's turn-taking self-evident at a glance.
3. **Pass 3 — Handshake weight.** The first handshake band was a solid moss success banner — it read as "the seller won." Finding: a close belongs to *both* parties, and the two-color system already encodes that. Rebuilt it as a split band (terracotta side / moss side) joined by a dashed two-color seam that draws in over 600ms, with both avatars facing the center chip "Shaken on £1,150." The rail preview of a closed deal reuses the same split bar at 8px height.

## What a design system for this thesis looks like

A COUNTER design system is organized around **who is speaking and where the deal stands**, not around component taxonomy. Its primitives: (1) two party colors — `--mine` terracotta and `--theirs` moss — applied consistently to bubbles, avatars, buttons, and the seam, so authorship is legible before a word is read; (2) the offer bubble with a four-state lifecycle (open → countered | accepted | expired) whose chips are the system's only status vocabulary; (3) money as inline emphasized text (600 weight, slight bump, tabular numerals) — never a boxed readout; (4) a warm neutral stage (stone / putty / paper) that stays out of the conversation's way; (5) motion limited to conversational physics — bubbles arrive like messages, fields nudge like a raised eyebrow, and the seam draws once at close. New features are designed by asking "what does the negotiation need at this moment?" and composing from these five primitives.

## The design system in Google's design.md standard

`design.md` in this folder follows Google's open **design.md** format (github.com/google-labs-code/design.md): a machine-readable YAML front-block declaring every token — colors, typography, spacing, radii, shadows — using the exact names and values found in `counter.css`, followed by prose sections describing component anatomy, states, and motion in plain language. The point of the standard is that a coding agent can consume the YAML to generate token-faithful UI without inventing values, while a human reads the prose to understand intent. Tokens in the YAML and in `:root` are kept 1:1; if a value changes in CSS it must change in design.md in the same commit. `design.html` renders the same tokens live as a self-contained style guide.

## Portable style emulation

To emulate COUNTER elsewhere: set a warm stone canvas (#EFEBE4) with putty insets (#E2DCD2) and warm-white panels (#FBF9F6); type everything in General Sans with warm charcoal (#262320) and taupe secondary (#857D72); assign one party terracotta-rust (#C4593A) and the other deep moss (#4F5D42) and apply those colors to every authored element (bubbles, avatars, actions) with tinted fills at roughly 15–20% chroma; render money inline at 600 weight with tabular numerals; keep radii at 14px for panels, 18px for bubbles with a 6px corner pointing at the author; shadows never exceed 0 1px 3px at 6% opacity; and animate only like a conversation — 220ms ease-out slide-up entries with a 2px spring settle, a 260ms horizontal nudge for corrections, and a single 600ms seam-draw when two parties agree.
