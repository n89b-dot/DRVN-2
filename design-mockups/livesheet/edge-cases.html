SHEET 97 — SPEC · THE CONTENT NOBODY DESIGNS FOR

When the content
fights back.

Prototypes are drawn with obedient data: four-word titles, three photos, tidy prices. Real sheets carry a 78-character part name, a $0 giveaway, a panorama, 200 build entries and a German microlabel. Each case below shows the same component twice — as designed, and as reality delivers it — with the rule that keeps the ruling straight.

Rule: every value column carries overflow-wrap: anywhere. An unbreakable token wraps mid-token — it never widens the tile, never triggers a horizontal scrollbar, and is never truncated with an ellipsis, because a half-shown part number is worse than an ugly wrap: it can be misread as complete. Rule: giant figures are tabular-nums and set in a clamp() that steps down at five digits, so $12,450 occupies the same box as $680 and no KPI tile reflows. 07 — ZERO, AND FREE GIVEAWAY
Free
Asking$0Rendered as the word "Free" in headline position, "$0" in ledgers
Buyer pays$18.40Shipping only — stated before checkout, never as a surprise

Rule: $0 is a real value, not an empty state — it never renders as "—". In display position it becomes the word "Free"; in any money column, ledger or receipt it stays "$0.00" so the arithmetic is auditable. A free part with non-zero shipping must show the buyer's actual total on the card, because "Free" plus a $18.40 surprise at checkout is the single fastest way to lose trust.

Rule: a computed average over an empty set is undefined, not zero — 0.0 must never render, and neither must an empty star row, because both read as "rated badly". 11 — NO PHOTO AT ALL

Rule: a missing photo becomes a dashed plate at the exact aspect ratio of a real one (3:2) so grids never reflow and no card is taller than its neighbours. It says what is missing in mono, shows no stock imagery, no manufacturer logo and no "image coming soon" — inventing a picture for a used part is a provenance lie. Listings without a photo rank below listings with one and the seller is told why.

Rule: the slot's aspect ratio is fixed by the layout and the image is centre-cropped with object-fit: cover — never letterboxed with bars, never stretched. The uncropped original is what opens in the lightbox and what the seller sees when choosing the cover shot, so nothing is lost, only framed. Sellers are told the crop is 3:2 at upload; a portrait shot is accepted, not rejected.

Rule: mono microlabels are allowed to wrap — they are never set white-space: nowrap, and layouts must survive a label growing 2.5× from English (the German worst case).
