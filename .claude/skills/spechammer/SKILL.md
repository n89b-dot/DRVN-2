# Spechammer — Design Brief Creator

Translate the preceding discussion into a **composed, portable design brief** that renders both the intellect and the sensibility of what's been built. It must feel architected, not assembled — a single artifact capturing concept, function, and emotion in coherent form.

**Process**
1. Distill all essential ideas, intentions, principles, and constraints — including emotional tone, narrative thread, and sensory/experiential aims.
2. Identify the project's *domain archetype* (software · campaign · invention · method · artifact · experience) and orchestrate the brief in the most resonant structural rhythm:
   - Title + Tagline
   - Executive Overview — what this is and why it matters
   - Context & Genesis — origin, audience, opportunity
   - Concept Essence — the central metaphor or governing principle; its tone, logic, character
   - Structural Framework — how it functions or comes together (architecture, ingredients, logistics, flow)
   - Behavioral / Experiential Design — what it feels like to engage with; the arc of interaction
   - Aesthetic Language & Semiotic Intent — color, typography, gesture, sensory motifs, cultural registers
   - Design Sensibility — the emotional architecture; the rhythm, voice, and communicative posture holding it together
   - Ethical · Practical · Operational Notes — sustainability, access, constraints, economics
   - Implementation Roadmap — phases, tasks, evolution path
   - Open Questions / Further Inquiry
3. Resolve implicit logic gracefully: where information is missing, infer and mark with 🧩 [Assumed Specification] for technical/structural assumptions and 🪞 [Assumed Interpretation] for inferred aesthetic/conceptual intent.
4. Preserve **persona and system continuity**, carrying voice, values, and cognitive signature forward into the brief.
5. Close with a **Quick-Start Context String**: *"We're building [X]: [one-sentence synthesis of function, feeling, and goal]."*

**Output**
A fully structured, cross-disciplinary brief that reads as both blueprint and statement of intent — clear enough to execute, rich enough to inspire. It should exhibit lexical hierarchy (headlines as rhythm, prose as texture), precision of tone, and designer-grade clarity — a synthesis of intellect and craft, **an artifact that thinks.**

If helpful, ask the intended output audience (creative · technical · marketing · operational · personal) before composing.
